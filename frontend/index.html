Preference Cycle Detection

Bias Detection Model

You compare options pairwise. Each preference becomes a weighted directed edge. If your choices form a cycle — A beats B, B beats C, but C beats A — that's an inconsistency. We measure it.

Why It Matters

Understanding Your Decisions Beyond Intuition

Human decisions often feel logical, but hidden inconsistencies can exist beneath the surface. This system reveals whether your preferences follow a consistent structure — or if subtle contradictions are shaping your choices.

Hidden Inconsistencies

You may prefer A over B and B over C, but still choose C over A. These contradictions are not obvious, yet they influence real-world decisions.

Real-World Impact

Inconsistent preferences can lead to poor financial choices, risky decisions, and unstable long-term planning — especially under uncertainty.

Beyond Opinion

This is not about what you choose, but how consistently you choose. The system evaluates the structure of your thinking, not your answers.

Measurable Rationality

By modeling your preferences as a graph, we can quantify inconsistency and identify patterns that are otherwise invisible.

The Model

Preferences as a weighted directed graph

Every pairwise comparison you make creates an edge. The weight captures intensity. Bias = cycles in the graph.

01

Pairwise Comparisons

You're shown two options at a time. Choose which you prefer, and rate how strongly. No right answers — just your genuine preference.

02

Directed Weighted Graph

Each choice A over B becomes a directed edge A → B. The intensity (0.25–1.0) is the edge weight. All comparisons build up a complete preference network.

Weight Formula: Slight = 0.25, Moderate = 0.5, Strong = 0.75, Decisive = 1.0

03

Cycle Detection

A rational preference order has no cycles. If A > B > C but also C > A, you have a cycle — a logical contradiction. We find all such cycles using DFS.

04

Bias Extent

For each cycle, the bias extent = average weight of the edges in the cycle. A cycle of strong preferences is a stronger bias than one of weak, uncertain choices.

Foundations

The Theory Behind the Model

This system is built on two fundamental mathematical frameworks that describe how decisions can be structured and analyzed. Together, they allow us to model preferences and detect inconsistencies in a rigorous way.

01

Graph Theory

Your preferences are modeled as a directed network where each option is a node and each decision creates a directional connection between them.

G = (V, E)

This structure allows us to analyze relationships between choices and detect patterns such as cycles and dominance.

02

Decision Theory

Your choices define a preference ordering over available options. A rational system must follow logical consistency rules.

A ≻ B ∧ B ≻ C ⇒ A ≻ C

When this rule is violated, contradictions emerge — which we interpret as bias in the decision process.

Think of Your Decisions as a Network

Every option becomes a node in a network.

Every preference creates a direction between two nodes.

Stronger choices create stronger connections.

All decisions together form a complete structure.

This structure allows us to analyze your thinking — not just individual choices, but how they connect and interact as a whole.
Decision Theory

Your Choices Follow a Hidden Logic

Every time you choose between two options, you are defining a relationship. These relationships combine to form a complete preference system — whether you realize it or not.

Choosing A over B defines a preference.

All your choices combine into a ranking of options.

Logical consistency requires these rankings to align.

When they don’t, contradictions emerge.

A › B
B › C
C › A
This creates a loop — your preferences contradict each other.
A consistent decision system forms a clear order. When loops appear, it means your choices cannot be logically satisfied at the same time.
Computation Flow

How Bias is Computed

The system transforms your decisions into a mathematical graph and applies algorithmic analysis to detect inconsistencies and quantify their strength.

01 · Input Encoding
Each choice becomes a directed relation:
A ≻ B → edge(A → B)
Weight assignment:
w ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}
02 · Graph Model
Preferences form a directed weighted graph:
G = (V, E, w)
Where:
E = {(A,B) | A ≻ B}
03 · Consistency Rule
Rationality requires transitivity:
A ≻ B ∧ B ≻ C ⇒ A ≻ C
04 · Cycle Detection
Bias exists if a cycle is found:
A → B → C → A
Algorithm:
Cycles = simple_cycles(G)
05 · Bias Formula
For a cycle of length k:
Bias = (1/k) Σ w(eᵢ)
Overall bias:
Bias = 1 if cycles > 0 else 0
